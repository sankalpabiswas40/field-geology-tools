    Help

    Record on ONE device only

    Stations are saved only on the device you record them on. iPad and iPhone do not share them. If you record on both, you get two separate sets with clashing station IDs. Pick one device for the whole trip.

    Daily routine

    1. Night before: charge the device and a power bank.
    2. Before leaving (with internet, once after any app update): open the app so it can update itself. Check the DEM appears and the station counter looks right.
    3. At the first stop: tap My location and wait until GPS accuracy is small (for example ± 5–10 m). Do this before switching on Airplane Mode: the first fix is much faster then. GPS still works in Airplane Mode.
    4. During the day: keep Airplane Mode on to save battery. The app works fully offline.
    5. Every evening: tap Export and save the files to Files (or send them somewhere safe), then make a Backup. The counter turns amber while some stations are not exported.

    Recording a station

    1. Tap New station. The ID (like D2-05), date and time fill in by themselves.
    2. Location: Use my location (GPS), or Place on map when GPS is poor (zoom in first, then tap the spot). You can also type latitude/longitude.
    3. The elevation comes from the DEM, never from GPS height.
    4. Choose the feature type. Repeat last copies the feature type and lithology of your previous station (never its attitude or location).
    5. Type strike and dip (right-hand rule, see below), or trend and plunge for a lineation. Check the big compass arrow points the way the bed really dips.
    6. Tap Save station. Mistakes are shown in red and nothing is saved until they are fixed.

    The right-hand rule

    Face along the strike direction. With the right-hand rule the bed always dips to your right. So dip direction = strike + 90°.

    Example: 045/30 dips 30° towards 135° (south-east). A bed dipping towards 315° (north-west) must be written 225/30, not 045/30.

    Hand trick: put your right hand flat on the bed with your fingers pointing down the dip. Your thumb points along the strike to record.

    The trace predictor

    It assumes the bed is a perfect flat plane through the station, with the strike and dip you measured, and shows where that plane meets the ground on the DEM.

    • Solid line: your measurement. Dashed lines: dip and strike ± the uncertainty in Trace settings.
    • On gentle slopes, or where the bed dips nearly parallel to the ground, a small dip error moves the line a long way.
    • If the real contact does not follow the line, the bed is not one flat plane there. That is evidence of folding or faulting, which is often the interesting part.
    • The DEM is about 30 m per cell, so small gullies and cliffs are smoothed out.

    Reading the stereonet

    • Dots are poles to planes, triangles are lineations, pink means TEST DATA.
    • Fold axis (red square): only for bedding. The red dashed great circle is the best-fit girdle.
    • "Only N poles": fewer than 6 bedding readings. No axis is given until you have more.
    • "One cluster, not a girdle": the poles bunch together, which usually means you only measured one limb. Measure the other limb too.
    • "Do not split clearly into two limb groups": an axis is given, but no interlimb angle. The fold may be rounded, or the readings are spread evenly.
    • Cylindrical fit: "very good" or "good" means the poles follow one great circle. "Fair" or "poor" means scatter, a non-cylindrical fold, or a mix of structures.
    • Two interlimb angles: (a) uses the two most different readings, so it shows the tightest part of the fold. (b) uses the average of each limb. Both get a Fleuty (1964) class.
    • Dips are always 0–90°, so an overturned limb looks upright. For tight or overturned folds, record younging and check the angles yourself.
    • Use antiform/synform unless you know which way the beds young.
